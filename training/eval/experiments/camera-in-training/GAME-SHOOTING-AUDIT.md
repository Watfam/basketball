# Audit: do our shooting plans build game shooters, or just shot counts?

Read from the real seeded content (drills, workouts, the Six-Week Shot Builder program, Film Room lessons and study sessions), loaded into a local database, with the app's own prescription code computing what a player is actually asked to do each week. Nothing was changed. "Prescribed" means what the drill text asks the player to do. "Camera" means what the app can observe: shots, make or miss, and the time each shot happened. The camera does not see footwork, speed, defenders or decisions, and nothing here claims it does.

## The finding in one paragraph

The plan is **not** stationary-heavy. Of the shots the Shot Builder prescribes, 14 to 27% are stationary (form work and free throws); the rest follow a sprint and catch or one to two dribbles. The risk you described is real in a different place: **volume is the only dial.** From week 1 to week 5 the same drills, variants, spots and targets repeat, and only the quantity rises (intermediate: 290 to 430 counted shots, +48%; the timed finishers get 5 seconds longer each week). Every rep is also predictable: fixed spot, fixed path, no cue, no defender, no decision, a rest between sets. A player who gets better at this will make more shots in practice. Whether that carries to a game is not something the plan, or the camera, can currently tell.

Answers to your first four questions:
1. **Stationary mechanics:** One-Hand Form Shooting, Form Shooting Progression, and Free Throw Reset (a stationary shot in a fatigue-and-routine setting). Everything else is built to look like a game shot.
2. **Explicit movement before the shot:** Catch-and-Shoot off the Pin-Down (a sprint, which my earlier single-category model recorded only as "catch-and-shoot" and so under-described). The pull-ups and step-back move through their dribbles. Beat the Clock moves between shots.
3. **Change of direction, off the dribble, preparing for a catch:** Elbow Pull-Up and the Pull-Up Series (off the dribble, both directions as separate variants), Wing Step-Back (a direction change, advanced level only), Pin-Down (prepare for the catch: "target hand up BEFORE the catch").
4. **Decisions, time pressure, game situations:** Time pressure only, in Beat the Clock. **No existing drill requires a decision, a read, a live defender or an unpredictable path.** The decision layer exists only in the Film Room, not in any drill.

## 3. What is measured today

Question 5. Every drill measures **repetitions and makes (or time), nothing about execution.** The cues ("land where you took off", "feet and hips turned to the rim as you catch") and the Film Room's "watch for" lists are the real quality criteria, and no screen records whether they were met. The camera adds make or miss and, from the shot times it already stores, when each shot happened. It does not see footwork, balance, release or defenders. (The experimental shot-path features can show arc consistency, which relates to "same arc every rep", but they are off, unvalidated and not used here.)

## 5. Where the camera helps and where it must not be believed

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| This drill's shots were made or missed, and how many | Camera, checked by the player |
| Makes in the first 60 seconds of a timed finisher | Computable from stored shot times; the clock is the app's timer |
| The drill was a pull-up, a catch-and-shoot, or a sprint and catch | Prescribed by the drill, chosen by the player. **Not observed.** |
| Footwork was right, feet were ready, the player landed where they took off | Not measured. Only the player or a coach can say. |
| Game speed, defender pressure, decision quality | Not measured, and no drill currently creates them |
| Performance in a game | No claim. Practice results are practice results. |

## 6. The three improvements with the most value

Not built. Each reuses what exists (plan entries and variants, Film Room lessons, camera makes, the workout log). Each is small enough to try on one program first.

### 1. Benchmarks at fixed difficulty, so progress cannot be bought with volume

- **Problem:** Week 1 says "log honest numbers" and week 6 says "compare these numbers to week 1", but the screen cannot compare fairly. The counts, and the finisher clocks, are different by then, and total shots is the number that visibly goes up.
- **Plan:** Mark three or four existing entries in each block as benchmarks (for example Catch-and-Shoot right wing, Elbow Pull-Up right side, Free Throw Reset, Beat the Clock at the three-point line). Their shot count or clock does not scale with the weekly volume step, and they are repeated in weeks 1, 3 and 6.
- **Camera:** Tracks the benchmark entries so the same number of shots is counted each time. For a clock, compare makes in the same window (for example the first 60 seconds), not the whole clock.
- **Progress:** One card, "Benchmarks: same drill, same spot, same shots", showing week 1 to week 6 with the range of normal shot-to-shot variation, and saying "not enough to call a change" when it is not. Total volume is shown as effort, never as improvement.
- **Film Room:** The week's existing lesson is attached to the benchmark drills it teaches, and "watched" is shown beside the result without implying cause.
- **Verified vs reported:** Makes, attempts and shot times are measured; "same drill, spot and count" is by definition of the plan. Nothing about games.
- **Needs from you:** Which entries are benchmarks, and whether the marker is a new yes/no column on the plan entry or a field inside the existing target data (I would use a column; it is one small migration).

### 2. A ten-second execution check, written from the Film Room's own "watch for" lines

- **Problem:** Make rate over 10 to 30 shots is noisy and can be luck; the skill is in the cues, and nothing records them.
- **Plan:** After a set, ask one or two questions taken from the drill's lesson (or its cues if there is no lesson): "Did you land where you took off?", "Were your feet turned to the rim before the catch?" Answers: Yes, Mostly, No, skippable. Stored in the same workout-log entry as the result. A coach can answer for a player (you, with your team).
- **Camera:** Unchanged (counts makes). The check is labelled "your check; the camera does not see footwork".
- **Progress:** Execution rate beside make rate at the same drill and benchmark. "Makes up, execution flat" is a warning that the gain may be luck or volume; "execution up first" is the leading sign.
- **Film Room:** The lesson's watch-for list becomes the checklist, which ties the video to the rep that follows it. Where no lesson exists, the drill's own cues are used, which also shows which drills lack a lesson.
- **Verified vs reported:** All of the check is player-reported or coach-reported and is labelled so.

### 3. A difficulty ladder built from variants of the existing drills

- **Problem:** The rungs after "predictable pull-up" do not exist: no decision, no cue, no defender, and the step-back is advanced only, while the library already teaches reads.
- **Plan:** Define rungs with the plan's own variants and levels, in this order: stationary, sprint and catch (Pin-Down), one dribble (Elbow), two dribbles or a combo, step-back (change of direction), **a cue decides the move** (a new variant of the Pull-Up Series taken from the existing Hennen lesson, no new system), then against a clock, then a partner or coach as the defender. The partner rung is set up by a person; the camera still only counts makes. The app suggests the next rung when the benchmark at the current rung holds at 30 or more shots across two sessions, never forces it, and you can override.
- **Camera:** Counts makes at each rung. The rung is labelled as set up by the drill, not seen by the camera.
- **Progress:** "Highest rung with a benchmark result" next to total shots. A player who takes 400 free throws stays on rung 1 and the screen says so.
- **Film Room:** Each rung gets its existing lesson: link Playing Without the Ball and the two Curry lessons to the Pin-Down, make sure Creating Separation is linked to Wing Step-Back (query above), link Hennen "React to Something" and the drop-coverage lessons to the cue and decision rungs. This uses relationships that mostly exist and need confirming, not new content.
- **Verified vs reported:** Makes only. Whether a "cue-decided" or "defended" rep felt like a game is the player's or coach's call.

**Order:** 1 first (it stops volume being mistaken for progress and is the least work), then 2, then 3, because 3 depends on the benchmarks in 1 to know when to move a player up.
